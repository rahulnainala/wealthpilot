# Mutual fund mechanics (India)

NAV (net asset value) is the per-unit price, computed daily after market
close. Orders before the 3:00 pm cutoff get that day's NAV (for equity
funds; liquid funds differ). NAV level is meaningless for comparing funds —
a ₹10 NAV fund is not "cheaper" than a ₹500 one; only returns matter.

Direct plans skip distributor commissions and cost roughly 0.5–1.0% less
per year than regular plans of the same fund — always prefer direct. Over
25 years, a 0.75% annual drag consumes about a fifth of the final corpus.

SEBI categories cap what funds may hold: large-cap (80% in top-100 stocks),
midcap (65% in 101–250), flexicap (65% equity, any caps), ELSS (80% equity,
3-year lock-in per installment, old-regime 80C benefit only), index funds
(95% in the index), arbitrage (equity-hedged, taxed as equity, debt-like
returns).

Exit loads: commonly 1% if redeemed within 365 days (equity). Plan
redemptions after the load window when possible.

Expense ratios compound silently: 0.2% for index funds vs 1.5–2.3% for
regular active plans. The difference is the single most controllable return
driver an investor has.

XIRR is the correct return measure for SIPs (money-weighted, handles each
installment's timing); CAGR fits lump sums. Comparing your SIP's XIRR to
the index's CAGR overstates or understates skill depending on market path.

Growth vs IDCW: IDCW ("dividend") payouts are your own NAV returned to you
and taxed at slab — choose growth and redeem when needed instead.

A fund's stated category, benchmark, expense ratio, and exit load are in
its scheme document; audit these yearly (the MF Audit tab automates this).
