# Goal-based investing principles WealthPilot follows

Each goal owns sleeves of assets with return/volatility assumptions; success
probability comes from Monte Carlo simulation (10,000 paths of monthly
geometric Brownian motion), not point estimates. A goal at 99% is
over-funded or long-dated; below ~70% needs more SIP, more time, or a lower
target.

Glide paths: equity-heavy while far from the deadline, de-risking into
arbitrage/short-debt 12–18 months before the money is needed — sequence risk
in the final stretch hurts more than average returns help. The Travel-2030
and Vehicle-2031 buckets both follow this.

Contribution discipline beats timing: a fixed monthly SIP into index funds
captures rupee-cost averaging; pausing SIPs in drawdowns is the single most
expensive behavioral error, since the cheapest units are bought at the
bottom. Lump sums (bonuses) are best deployed immediately for horizons over
5 years; splitting over 3–6 months only reduces regret, not expected value.

Repositioning legacy holdings: prefer selling winners at predefined profit
thresholds (removes discretion), harvest LTCG within the annual exemption,
and route proceeds by the plan's split rather than ad hoc. Never let a
tactical hold (waiting for +10%) survive past the plan's hard deadline —
time-boxing converts a drifting position into a decision.

Emergency fund (6 months of expenses), debt clearance, and health insurance
precede all bucket investing — a forced redemption in a drawdown destroys
more value than any allocation optimizes.
