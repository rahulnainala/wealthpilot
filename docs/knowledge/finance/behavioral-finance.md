# Behavioral finance — the biases that cost real money

Loss aversion: losses hurt about twice as much as equivalent gains please.
It makes investors hold losers ("it'll come back") and sell winners early.
The exit plan's +10% threshold and hard Dec-2027 deadline exist precisely
to replace this emotion with a rule.

Recency bias: extrapolating the last 6–12 months forever — buying midcaps
after a rally, abandoning SIPs after a crash. The correction: allocation
targets and automatic monthly SIPs that ignore the news.

Anchoring: treating your buy price as meaningful. The market does not know
your cost basis; the only question is whether the asset earns its place at
TODAY's price. "Waiting to break even" on a bad holding is anchoring plus
loss aversion compounding.

Disposition effect + endowment: valuing what you own above identical
alternatives. Test: "would I buy this position today at this size?" If no,
the exit plan applies.

Overconfidence and action bias: trading more correlates with earning less
(costs, taxes, mistiming). A monitoring dashboard's job is to enable
INACTION with confidence — most days the right move is nothing.

Herding and FOMO: crypto 2021, smallcaps 2017/2024 — inflows peak at tops.
A pre-committed plan (a fixed SIP split, bucket routing) is the antidote;
deviations require writing down a reason first.

Mental accounting: money is fungible, but bucket structures exploit this
bias FOR you — labeling money "Travel 2030" makes raiding it psychologically
expensive, which is the point.

Sunk cost: money already lost is irrelevant to what you do next. Cinema
rig, legacy stocks — decisions run forward from today.

Why SIP discipline beats timing: missing just the 10 best days of a decade
roughly halves equity returns, and those days cluster inside crashes —
exactly when fear says exit. Automation removes the decision; the pause
button is the most expensive feature of any SIP.
