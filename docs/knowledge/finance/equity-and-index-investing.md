# Equity and index investing

An index fund holds every stock in an index (Nifty 50, Nifty Midcap 150) at
index weights, so returns equal the market's minus a small expense ratio.
Most active large-cap funds in India underperform their index over 10 years
after costs, which is why the plan routes new money to index funds first.
Tracking error measures how closely a fund follows its index — under 0.2%
annualized is good for a Nifty 50 fund; prefer lower tracking difference and
larger AUM for liquidity.

Flexicap funds let a manager move across large/mid/small caps. They earn a
place (like Parag Parikh Flexi Cap) when the manager has a long record of
downside protection and reasonable costs — but they carry manager risk that
an index fund does not.

Midcap indexes (Nifty Midcap 150) return more over long horizons with much
deeper drawdowns — appropriate for goals 7+ years away, like Vehicle 2031,
never for money needed within 5 years.

Direct stocks concentrate risk: a single company can permanently lose 50–90%
regardless of the market. Two stocks in the same sector (BPCL and IOC, both
oil marketing PSUs) behave like one bet — correlation above 0.8 means
diversification on paper only. The portfolio's own history shows PSU-energy
names moving together at 0.9+ correlation.

Dividend yield is not free income — the price drops by the dividend on
ex-date, and dividends are taxed at slab rates in India, making high-dividend
PSU strategies tax-inefficient versus growth for anyone above the 20% slab.

Rebalancing: check allocation against targets yearly or on ±5% drift bands;
sell the overweight, buy the underweight. It enforces buy-low/sell-high
mechanically and controls risk more than it adds return.
