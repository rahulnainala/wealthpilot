# Indian market history — drawdowns and recoveries

Every equity plan should be stress-read against what markets have actually
done. Sensex/Nifty major drawdowns:

- 2008 global financial crisis: Sensex fell ~60% (21,000 → 8,200) over 14
  months; recovered the peak by late 2010 (~2 years).
- 2020 COVID crash: Nifty fell ~38% in 5 weeks (12,400 → 7,600) — the
  fastest bear market ever; recovered in 9 months, then doubled.
- 2011 (European debt), 2013 (taper tantrum), 2015–16 (China/NPA), 2018
  (NBFC/IL&FS): 15–25% corrections, one every 2–3 years on average.
- Harshad Mehta 1992: −55%, took 7+ years to durably recover.

Base rates worth internalizing: a 10% correction is a normal YEAR, not a
crisis; 30–40% bears arrive roughly once a decade; intra-year drawdowns
average ~15% even in positive years. The portfolio's own 3-month max
drawdown (a few percent) is calm weather, not a ceiling.

Holding-period math (Nifty history): 1-year returns range roughly −55% to
+100%; 7-year holding periods have almost never been negative; 10-year
rolling returns have run ~10–14% CAGR. This is why the goal design maps
horizons to equity share — Travel 2030 and Vehicle 2031 can hold heavy
equity now, but must de-risk as the date approaches (a 2008-scale event in
the final year would otherwise cut the corpus roughly in half with no time
to recover).

Sectoral notes for this portfolio: PSU/energy names amplify these cycles
(higher beta to commodity and policy swings) and cluster together — the
0.9+ BPCL~IOC correlation means a sector event hits the whole legacy block
at once, which is the concentration the sell-off plan is unwinding.

Crashes are also when SIPs earn their keep: units bought in 2008 and March
2020 produced the best forward returns of their decades. The plan's answer
to "market is falling" is: continue SIPs, check Phase 0 buffers, touch
nothing else.
