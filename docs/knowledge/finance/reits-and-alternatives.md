# REITs, InvITs, gold and alternatives (India)

REITs (Embassy, Mindspace, Brookfield) own income-producing offices and
must distribute 90% of cash flows. Distributions are a MIX: interest (slab
tax), dividend (often exempt), and return of capital (reduces cost basis,
taxed at sale). In a 30% bracket the post-tax yield is far below headline —
often 5–6% versus the advertised 7–8%. Units trade like stocks; prices are
rate-sensitive (rise in yields → REIT prices fall) and occupancy-sensitive.
BIRET-RR style rights entitlements are separate tradable instruments with
expiry — exercise or sell before lapse.

REITs in this portfolio (e.g. EMBASSY) are legacy positions
being exited per the sell-off plan; they cluster with each other and with
rate cycles, hurting effective diversification.

InvITs (PowerGrid, IRB) are the infrastructure cousins — higher yield,
more concentrated asset risk, similar mixed taxation.

Gold: efficient holdings are Gold ETFs/funds (HDFCGOLD) or SGBs. SGBs pay
2.5% annual interest and are capital-gains tax-free if held to 8-year
maturity — strictly better than ETFs when available in the horizon. Gold's
role is 5–10% as an equity-crisis hedge (it rose in 2008 and 2020 crashes);
more than that drags long-run returns since gold's real return ≈ 0–2%.
Jewellery is consumption (20–30% making charges), not investment.

Crypto: taxed at flat 30% + 1% TDS in India, no loss offset — treat as
speculation capped well under 5% if held at all; it is not part of the
The plan.

P2P lending, ULIPs, endowment policies: high-cost or high-risk structures
that mix insurance with investment or chase yield; the plan excludes them.
Insurance is bought as pure protection (term/health), never as investment.
