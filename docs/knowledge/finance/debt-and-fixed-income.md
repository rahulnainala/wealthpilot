# Debt and fixed income (India)

Fixed deposits give a contracted rate, DICGC insurance up to ₹5 lakh per
bank, and slab-rate taxation of interest annually — simple, safe, and
tax-inefficient in high brackets.

Debt mutual funds hold bonds. Two risks: duration risk (long-maturity bonds
fall when rates rise — a 7-year duration fund drops ~7% for a 1% rate rise)
and credit risk (defaults, as in the 2018–2020 credit events that gated
several funds). For goal money, prefer short-duration or money-market funds
with high-credit portfolios (AAA/sovereign) — you are storing value, not
reaching for yield. Post-April-2023, debt fund gains are taxed at slab
regardless of holding period, removing their old indexation edge.

Arbitrage funds buy a stock and sell its future simultaneously, earning the
spread — market-neutral, debt-like ~6–7% returns, but taxed as EQUITY
(12.5% LTCG beyond ₹1.25L after 12 months). This tax treatment is why the
The plan parks near-goal money (Travel from 2028–29, Vehicle from 2030)
in arbitrage rather than debt funds for high-bracket investors.

Liquid funds settle in 1 business day and suit emergency funds beyond the
first month of expenses (keep month one in a savings account). Overnight
funds are the lowest-risk category.

Glide path rule the plan follows: equity while the goal is far, then move
12–18 months of the goal's value into arbitrage/short-debt starting 12–18
months before the date — sequence risk (a crash just before withdrawal)
destroys more than late-stage equity returns add.

EMIs: paying 9%+ loan interest while investing at an expected 12% is a
leveraged equity bet, not a plan. Clear high-interest debt first (Phase 0);
a car EMI at 8–9% is tolerable only because the car is consumption already
committed, not an investment.
