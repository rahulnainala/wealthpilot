# Insurance and protection (why Phase 0 gates investing)

Insurance is bought for protection, never as an investment. Mixing the two
(endowment, money-back, ULIPs) yields 4–5% returns with heavy surrender
penalties — strictly worse than term insurance plus index funds.

Term life insurance: pure death cover. Size at 10–15× annual income (or
enough to clear liabilities plus fund dependents' goals). A 30-year-old
non-smoker buys ₹1 crore cover for roughly ₹10–15k/year. Only needed when
someone depends on your income; a single person with no dependents can
defer it.

Health insurance is non-negotiable before investing: one hospitalization
(₹3–15 lakh in a metro) can wipe out years of SIPs — that is why Phase 0
requires it before any bucket money flows. Sizing: at least ₹5–10 lakh base
cover per person, topped with a super top-up (e.g. ₹50 lakh above a ₹10
lakh deductible) which is cheap because claims rarely reach it. Employer
cover vanishes with the job — never rely on it alone. Buy young: premiums
lock lower, waiting periods (2–4 years for pre-existing conditions) finish
before you need them.

Emergency fund: 6 months of expenses (Phase 0's second gate). Month one in
savings, the rest in liquid funds or sweep FDs. It exists so a job loss or
repair never forces selling equity in a drawdown — the most expensive
possible redemption. It is insurance you pay for in foregone returns, and
worth it.

Disability/critical-illness riders cover income loss the base policies do
not; consider once income is substantial.

The The plan's Phase 0 (clear high-interest debt → 6-month emergency fund
→ health insurance) exists because each item, missing, can force liquidation
of goal money at the worst time. Buckets stay empty until Phase 0 is done —
by design, not delay.
