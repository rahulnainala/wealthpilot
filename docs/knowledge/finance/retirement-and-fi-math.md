# Retirement and financial independence math

The core FI question: what corpus lets withdrawals replace expenses
forever? The classic answer is 25× annual expenses (the "4% rule"), but
that derives from US data. For India — higher inflation (~6%), different
sequence risk — 30–35× is safer for early retirement, or a withdrawal rate
of 3–3.5%.

Always compute in future rupees: ₹12 lakh/year of expenses today at 6%
inflation is ₹38 lakh/year in 20 years; a 30× corpus target is then ₹11.5
crore, not ₹3.6 crore. The FI Corpus goal's ₹1.5 crore placeholder is a
milestone, not the endpoint — at ₹15,000/month SIP it is deliberately
underfunded (the app's simulation shows ~0% probability), funded properly
only after Travel and Vehicle conclude their SIP claims.

Sequence-of-returns risk: two retirees with identical average returns can
end ruined or rich depending on whether the crash came early or late in
withdrawal. Defenses: 2–3 years of expenses in debt/arbitrage at
retirement, flexible withdrawals (skip inflation raises after down years),
and equity glide-down approaching the date — the same logic the goal
buckets use for Travel 2030.

Compounding arithmetic worth memorizing: at 12% nominal, money doubles
every ~6 years (rule of 72); ₹1 lakh/month SIP for 20 years at 12% builds
~₹10 crore; starting 5 years later halves it. Real (inflation-adjusted)
equity returns in India have historically run 5–7%.

EPF/PPF/NPS: EPF (12%+12% salary-linked, ~8% tax-free) and PPF (₹1.5L/yr
cap, ~7% tax-free, 15-year lock) are the debt backbone many Indian FI plans
use; NPS adds an 80CCD(1B) ₹50k deduction but locks until 60 with annuity
requirements. None of these are currently part of the The plan's buckets,
which run on market instruments for flexibility around big life changes —
a deliberate liquidity choice, revisit if the move plan changes.
