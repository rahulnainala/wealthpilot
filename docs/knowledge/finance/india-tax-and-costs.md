# Indian equity & mutual fund taxation and costs (FY 2025-26 regime)

Listed equity and equity mutual funds: gains on holdings over 12 months are
long-term (LTCG), taxed at 12.5% above the ₹1.25 lakh annual exemption.
Short-term (≤12 months) gains are taxed at 20%. Selling legacy stocks in
tranches across financial years can keep LTCG under the exemption.

Debt mutual funds bought after 1 Apr 2023 are taxed at slab rates regardless
of holding period. Gold ETFs/funds follow the post-2023 rules as well —
check current-year rules before acting; this note is a planning heuristic,
not tax advice.

ELSS funds carry a 3-year lock-in per SIP installment and give an 80C
deduction only under the old tax regime — under the new regime the deduction
is gone, which is why new money should prefer a non-ELSS equity fund unless
the old regime is elected.

Costs that quietly compound: expense ratios (direct plans typically 0.3–1.0%
for equity index/flexicap; regular plans ~0.75% higher — always prefer
direct), STT on delivery trades (0.1%), and exit loads (commonly 1% within
365 days for equity funds). A 1% annual cost drag reduces a 12% CAGR corpus
by roughly 25% over 25 years.

REIT distributions are a mix of dividend, interest, and return of capital,
each taxed differently; the interest component is taxed at slab, which often
makes REITs less attractive in high brackets than their headline yield.
